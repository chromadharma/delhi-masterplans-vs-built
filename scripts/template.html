Three Plans for Delhi

Three official land-use plans for Delhi, aligned on one map and laid over satellite-derived building footprint every five years from 1975 to 2020, with projections to 2030. The question is simple: did the city grow where it was drawn?

Prepared by Sahasrik Ragani · September 2026
2020observed

Finding 1

About half of new building went up beyond the plan's line

For the two older plans, I drew each plan's urban limit: everything it zoned for housing, work, institutions, parks, the airport or cantonment. Then I measured new building footprint, only counting land at least 400 m outside that line so alignment error cannot manufacture the result.

Finding 2

Each plan zoned more of the city, and the 2041 draft still keeps a green belt

With colours made comparable, planned housing, work, institutional and park land goes from about 392 km² on the 1962 sheet to 441 km² in 2001 and 605 km² in the 2041 draft. The 2041 draft also leaves about 390 km² in six land-pooling zones to be planned later. The rural edge is not erased: 210 km², 14% of Delhi, is still green belt.

km² within each sheet's coverage of Delhi: 1962 ≈ 1,010, 2001 ≈ 952, 2041 = 1,482. The two older sheets leave out the far north and west. The 2001 sheet draws the green belt as uncoloured hatching, so it appears here as zero.

Finding 3

Part of the 2041 draft's green land is already built on

The 2041 draft is drawn as vectors, so its green belt, Ridge and parks are exact. Taking only the interior of each class (100 m in from its edge), I counted land whose 100 m satellite cell is at least one-fifth covered by buildings.

The draft itself maps 106.6 km² of unauthorized colonies. 10.7 km² of that sits on land it zones green belt and 3.8 km² on the Ridge regional park.

Corrections

What the first-pass reading got wrong

  • The "georeferenced" TIFFs weren't georeferenced. Each whole sheet, legend and margins included, was stretched into Delhi's bounding box. Here every sheet is fitted to real control points instead.
  • The 1962 plan does not cover all of Delhi. It is titled Delhi Urban Area and covers about two-thirds of today's territory.
  • A colour means different things on different sheets. On the 1962 sheet, housing runs from yellow to brown by density and heavy industry is dark maroon. Counting "yellow pixels" across years compares different things.
  • A plan shows proposals, not what happened. "Encroachment is visible" or "extreme congestion" can't be read off a land-use plan. That's why the building-footprint layer is here.
  • 1962 did map the floodplain. Its legend has an explicit "Floodable Land" class.
  • The 2041 draft has six land-pooling zones: N, P-I, P-II, K-I, L and J. The earlier list missed P-I.
Method and limits

Alignment. The 2041 draft PDF holds 4,347 vector land-use polygons. Its silhouette was fitted to the OpenStreetMap boundary of Delhi: typical error 66 m, fitted scale within 0.03% of the sheet's scale bar. The 2001 scan was fitted to its drawn territory boundary plus three landmarks: boundary error around 50 m, landmarks 130–300 m, scale matching the scale bar. The 1962 scan uses ten control points (monuments, boundary corners, a rail line), with residuals of 40–370 m and a held-out check at the Qutub of 445 m. The 1962 base map was surveyed and hand-drawn before satellite imagery, so its placement can be a few hundred metres off. That is fine at city scale but not for claims about individual plots.

Classes. The scans were colour-enhanced (median filter, saturation and local-contrast boost), clustered, and mapped by hand to seven common classes. Line work was filled from neighbouring pixels. Against visual labels on stratified random points, 1962 scored 81% (42 points) and 2001 scored 86% (36 points). Residential and commercial/industrial were correct in every sample; most misses were parks vs public land and water. 2041 classes come straight from the vectors. Commercial and industrial are merged because the 1962 maroons can't be told apart reliably.

Building footprint. From the EU's Global Human Settlement Layer, GHS-BUILT-S R2023A: building surface at 100 m for 1975–2020, with 2025 and 2030 as model projections. Surface means roof area, not urban land, so 290 km² of footprint in 2020 corresponds to a much larger built-up city.

Urban limits. A 400 m morphological closing of all planned non-rural uses, with holes filled. The airport, cantonment and other hatched tracts were outlined by hand, because hatching can't be classified by colour.
